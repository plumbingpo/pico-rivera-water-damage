What Usually Goes Wrong in Sacramento Homes

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. Our crews in Sacramento follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in Sacramento, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Sacramento.

What to Do Before Our Sacramento Crew Arrives

People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Sacramento jobs, with the better option for each.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. It is an electric shock risk and it will ruin the machine; use towels and a mop. Leave ceiling fixtures off if the ceiling above is wet, and keep away from any part that droops. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Avoid cranking up the heating, as warmth plus moisture encourages mold.

Do shut off the water, open cabinet doors and closets so air can reach them, and hang up wet fabrics and cushions to drip. Photograph each room before you shift a single item. Keep kids and animals out until it has been checked. And do make the call early, so Pico Rivera Restoration can have a crew on the way to Sacramento while you do the rest.

Simple Ways to Keep a Sacramento Home Dry

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a Sacramento homeowner.

Pipes and fittings top the list. Watch your water bill and your water pressure, because an unexplained rise in either can point to a hidden leak or stress on the pipes. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. Third is the outside of the building. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Going away for more than a few days? Close the main valve before you go, or have a neighbor look in. Homeowners in Sacramento can ask Pico Rivera Restoration about any of this. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.

Drying Out a Sacramento Property: What to Expect

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. Our Sacramento technicians carry probe meters for wood and wallboard, scanning meters that read through finishes, and infrared cameras that pick up damp areas as cooler spots.

Not everything dries at the same rate. Carpet and drywall respond quickly, while framing lumber, subfloor, hardwood and concrete hold on much longer. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. Most jobs finish in 3 to 5 days, and if yours will take longer you will hear it from us at the start.

The expensive error is pulling equipment too soon. Damp materials sealed behind new finishes may mold, buckle or delaminate months afterward. So for every Sacramento job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.
