Attic Water Damage Help in Pico Rivera

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Pico Rivera inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Under the Roof Deck

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.
