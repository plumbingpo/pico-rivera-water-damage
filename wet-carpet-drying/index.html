Wet Carpet Drying Help in Pico Rivera

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In Pico Rivera, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

What We Do

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
