Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Pico Rivera

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

Signs to Look For

  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Water That Is Not Clean

Water under a house is often not clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Weakened Floor Framing

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Settling Under the House

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.
